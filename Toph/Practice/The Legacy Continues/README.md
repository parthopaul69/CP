## Problem Statement


		<h1>
			
				E.
			
			The Legacy Continues
		</h1>

		
			<p>Rakib vai and Nandi vai are phenomenal coders. Having been the ideal trainees of the legendary Sohom vai and Apu vai, they have now stepped up as the senior mentors to train the next generation of AUST ACM.</p>
<p>To prepare the freshers for the upcoming ICPC regionals, Rakib vai and Nandi vai are organizing an internal 2-person team contest. There are exactly $N$ students participating (where $N$ is always even), and the $i$-th student has a individual coding strength of $S_i$.</p>
<p>A team's total strength is simply the sum of both members' individual strengths. Rakib vai and Nandi vai want the contest to be fierce and balanced. If they pair up two weak coders, that team will be crushed and lose motivation. To prevent this, the seniors want to arrange the $N/2$ pairs perfectly so that the <strong>minimum total strength among all formed teams is as large as possible</strong>.</p>
<p>Can you help Rakib vai and Nandi vai figure out what this optimal lowest team strength will be?</p>


			
				
					<div role="heading" aria-level="3" class="pview__sectionhead">Input</div>
					<ul>
<li>
<p>The first line contains a single even integer $N$ — the number of students.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>The second line contains $N$ space-separated integers $S_1, S_2, \dots, S_N$, where $S_i$ is the coding strength of the $i$-th student.</p>
</li>
</ul>

				
				
					<div role="heading" aria-level="3" class="pview__sectionhead">Constraints</div>
					<ul>
<li>
<p>$2 \le N \le 10^5$ ($N$ is guaranteed to be even)</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>$1 \le S_i \le 10^9$</p>
</li>
</ul>

				
				
				
					<div role="heading" aria-level="3" class="pview__sectionhead">Output</div>
					<p>Print a single integer: the maximum possible value of the minimum team strength.</p>

				
			
		

		
		
			<div role="heading" aria-level="3" class="pview__sectionhead">Example</div>
